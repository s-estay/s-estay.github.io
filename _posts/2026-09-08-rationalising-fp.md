The usual question is: How do you know when your collection is complete? How do you know when you have enough? For some the answer is one. For others there are no limits. For me it is something in between. This entry is an effort to try to rationalise my collecting and use of fountain pens.

So what do I do with my pens? I do journal and write letters. But it is not that simple. Not after I have been in the fountain pen rabbit hole for more than one year. To overcome all the different variables I have come to narrow down the paper I use to just two: Midori MD and Tomoe River. Easy enough. Inks on the other hand are more complicated. They can have wet or dry flow, they can be easy or hard to clean, and they come in so many different colours it is mind blowing. I tend to reach for blues, teals and greens for the most part, and like to use reds in my letters, so I guess that is a good starting point. Pens are the most expensive part of the hobby and for that reason only I want to keep a healthy collection size in check. A good number is 18 just because I own three pouches (10, 6 and 2) and I have decided that 18 is a valid number in this irrational world.

## Journaling: Blues and teals
My most used category. The Lamy 2000 EF is great and has been inked with Taccia Sabimidori for a while. The Lamy 2000 M is currently out of rotation since I haven't found a good ink pairing. The Capless M is a true monoliner, something I like and I don't like. At the moment inked with Pilot Syo-Ro, a favourite summer teal. Capless nibs are bouncy and wet, and will display sheening. The Sailor B is my latest acquisition, a great nib, smooth, wet and controlled.
- Lamy 2000 Black 14k EF ⭐️
- Lamy 2000 Black 14k M
- Pilot Capless Seashore 18k M
- Sailor Pro Gear Black GT 21k B ⭐️

## Journaling: Greens
Only two pens for the greens. And both are great. The Sailor MF has the characteristic Sailor feedback that helps me with my cursive. Currently inked with Sailor Takiwa-Matsu. The Lamy 2000 Pine Green is my favourite of the two, a great writer, smooth and just-right wet. Forever inked with Sailor Rikyucha. The greens I usually go for are warm and muted, not too bright or cold like Pilot Shin-Ryoku. 
- Sailor Pro Gear Ceremonial Matcha 21k MF ⭐️
- Lamy 2000 Pine Green 14k F ⭐️

## Journaling: Reds and browns
Another Medium Capless that I don't totally like. Yet I have decided to keep it just because it is the best match for Pilot Momiji, a great reddish pink with golden sheen, a happy red. Momiji doesn't work on my Lamy 2000 pens, as they are not as wet. The Lamy 2000 OM is a great nib that allows for elegant cursive and line variation. Pilot Syun-Gyo is an ok match, need to find a better one.
- Pilot Capless Red Coral 18k M
- Lamy 2000 Brown 14k OM ⭐️

## Journaling: Headers (purples)
I discovered I only like purples in the headers. Purple is a good shade that gives nice contrast to blues, teals and greens. In this category I've used Kaweco Summer Purple and Pilot Yama-Budo for the longest time, lately leaning more towards dusty Summer Purple in my Kaweco Sport B. Kaweco premium steel nibs are surprisingly smooth for what they sell for.
- Kaweco Frosted Sport Sweet Banana steel B ⭐️

## Letter writing
While all my journaling is done in Midori MD notebooks, I like to use Tomoe River sheet paper for my letters. TR is very good at displaying shading, sheening and shimmer, so that is why I like to use all the funky inks here. Besides, my penpals appreciate beautiful inks. The Kaweco Sport can be totally disassemble and cleaned after using shimmer inks on them. A broad nib is best, smooth and wet.
- Kaweco Frosted Sport Sweet Banana steel B (shimmer inks)
- Kaweco Frosted Sport Natural Coconut steel B (shimmer inks)
- Lamy 2000 Black 14k M (blues and teals)
- Lamy 2000 Brown 14k OM (reds and browns) ⭐️
- Sailor Pro Gear Black GT 21k B (blues and teals) ⭐️

## Pigmented inks
While I want shimmer inks in easy to disassemble pens, I want pigmented inks in easy to flush pens with good cap seal. This is because I like to ink my pens with shimmer inks just enough to do what I want to do, and then clean them. Pens inked with pigmented inks may stay inked for longer periods of time, risking drying up. I don't use pigmented inks too often though. 
- Platinum 3776 Century Black Diamond 14k M (great cap seal) ⭐️
- Platinum 3776 Century Bourgogne RT 14k B (great cap seal)

## Drawing: Black
This last category is a smaller endeavour of mine: cross-hatching drawing. Beside drawing, the Sailor F is my go-to pen when I need to write in bad paper. It is forever inked with Pilot Take-Sumi. The Century has a great cap seal and it is my dedicated pen for Platinum Carbon Black.
- Sailor Pro Gear Black RT 21k F ⭐️
- Platinum 3776 Century Black Diamond 14k M ⭐️
