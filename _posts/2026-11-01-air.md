title: Air by Christian Kracht (2025)
tags: [books]
---

Two parallel stories: one in our reality, the other in a fantasy world. Paul is a Swiss interior designer living in a small cabin on Orkney, where he leads a peaceful life by the sea. He has a gift for finding the perfect colour for a room. One day he receives an unusual commission in Norway: to coat the cavernous interior of a data centre in Stavanger in the "perfect white". He accepts, and during a solar storm at the facility, he vanishes into thin air.

The fantasy world feels like being inside a Ghibli film; I loved it. There we follow a girl called Ildr, from the morning she is out hunting deer and, mistaking an oddly dressed stranger for one, shoots him with an arrow. Unknowingly, Ildr becomes entangled in the stranger's flight from the murderous soldiers of the Duke of Tviot. They flee south together, where the weather grows colder and the soil turns dry and arid. After many days of hard walking they finally reach the stone city above the Frozen Sea, rumoured to lie beyond the duke's reach. There, life is very different from what they knew, and at the same time peaceful and simple.

The plot is not especially convoluted until you notice how people disappear in one place and reappear in another, with the reader offered no explanation. Quite right, too: that is not the point of the book, but rather the turning point at which we begin to realise that one world is the mirror image of the other.

Heavy in symbolism and parallels, this is a book that rewards more than one reading, all the more so given its slender length of barely 200 pages.

Final rating: 4 one-eyed cats out of 5.
