title: The City and Its Uncertain Walls by Haruki Murakami (2023)

What is this book about? That is the question that has visited me during my time listening to this lengthy audiobook. To be stuck in time, in the best time of our lives, when we were happiest? Threads of decisive moments lead to this magical instant, a sharp point floating above every other moment, past and future. To be unknowingly headed towards it, as prepared as you will ever be, to experience your life’s highest, most significant instant. To slowly recede from it, living a life of sick nostalgia and longing. Is this book about that?

Close to the end of the book, he travels in a dream to that moment in the past, when he was together with his true love. Holding hands, standing by the river on that distant summer day, she whispered, *"Didn’t you know that? The two of us are nothing more than someone else’s shadows."* Could this perhaps be the realisation that their love, or even their selves, were never truly their own but just reflections of something, or someone, else?

For me, it is easy to admit I miss my younger years spent with my first love. A feeling I will always treasure. Yet here I am, almost as old as the protagonist, living past those years, making the most of the present. Scarred for sure, as nobody makes it this far without scars.

Moving past the book’s meaning, I would like to touch on how it felt to read it. Murakami is one of those authors who can turn paragraphs of apparent nothingness into a hypnotic rhythm of slow repetition, questioned at first, then accepted. Like in poetry, form steps in front of meaning. After 100 pages, I gave up searching for logic and surrendered to the rhythm. What I found was the saddest story of longing, a search for identity and meaning, wasted years of nothing, a warning tale for those who passively wait for better days when life itself is there, right in front of you, ready to be seized.

One Murakami book a year is enough though, I don’t want to poke at my broken psyche too often. I wonder if I will remember this book differently in a few years. Today, as it is, I will give it three freshly baked blueberry muffins out of five.
