title: Trophy by Gaea Schoeters (2020)
tags: [books]
---

A white American with the telling name Hunter White travels to Africa to hunt a black rhino. But what is left to kill once the Big Five are already hanging on your living room wall? When Hunter hears whispers of a "Big Six", the expedition takes a sinister turn. In Trophy, Schoeters relentlessly explores the hunting instinct until nothing remains but the absurd. The result is an adventure novel with uncomfortable questions about human nature.

Hunter's sharp focus during a hunt, every sense heightened until he feels more animal than man; beside it, the native way of hunting, closer to nature and steeped in respect. It is a beautiful image of a simple idea: humans hunting for food, as one creature among many, part of the ecosystem. Between those two ways of hunting, the whole novel quietly turns.

The final hunt turns into a nightmarish descent into paranoia, a fever dream. Schoeters wears her inspiration openly: like Conrad's Heart of Darkness, this is a book about colonialism, about what happens when humans are left unchecked and their desires are let loose, their dark thoughts left to run wild.

What lingers most is her refusal to judge. She does not condemn Hunter, and she does not celebrate him; she just shows us. A white man taking lives in Africa can never be a good thing, right? Yet Hunter argues his truth so persuasively that I caught myself rooting for him until the very end. Schoeters offers no verdict, and the novel is all the more unsettling for it.

Thrilling, with superb pace and matching character depth. Final verdict: 4 eland antelopes out of 5.
