title: Never Let Me Go by Kazuo Ishiguro (2005)
tags: [books]
---

In a contemporary dystopian world, we follow a group of children somewhere in the United Kingdom. They live at a boarding school where they attend class, make friends and, as they grow, fall in love. The story unfolds subtly, revealing only gradually the dystopian aspects that make the world surrounding these children different and quietly unsettling, and we as readers are kept in the dark just as much as they are. The children are apparently parentless, so it falls upon the teachers to answer existential questions about their origins and purpose in life. They are told they have a very important role in society and must take care of their health. The first cracks in the fabric of reality show in the teachers' odd reactions whenever a student does or asks something unexpected.

When they finish school, they move to a new place to continue their education. By this point they more or less know their purpose and have come to accept it, thanks to an effective indoctrination of expected values: they are clones, created to be organ donors for people in the outside world. As a reader, their acceptance of a doomed existence is heartbreaking, and yet they manage to find meaning and even happiness along the way.

The story is told by Kathy, a carer who visits her old friends in the final stages of their lives. We gradually discover that these people are dying because their bodies have given up after a series of organ donations. The final twist is the revelation of the controversial cloning programme the children were part of, and of the failed movement that once tried to give them a more humane upbringing, which explains all the secrecy surrounding their childhood.

A sad book, a beautiful book. Final verdict: 4 cassette tapes out of 5.
