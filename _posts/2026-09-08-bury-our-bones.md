"Bury Our Bones in the Midnight Soil" tells the story of three women connected through time, through centuries. We start with María in Spain 1532, a girl that, in her attempt to leave her family and see the world, gets married to a noble man that turns out to be violent and cold to her. To him, she is just a vessel where to put his heir. María doesn't want this life, doesn't want to be told what to do. One day she meets a mysterious widow. Her curiosity for the widow grows as time passes. The widow teaches María to read and to keep herself not pregnant. One day the widow asks María if she wants to be free, to which she says yes. What María didn't know was how dramatically her whole life would change after that day.

In the first half of the book we also get to know Alice, a Scottish young woman living in the USA 2019. Alice has moved to Boston to study. A great part of her daily thoughts are dedicated to her family, and especially her sister, back in Scotland. One night, at a university party, Alice meets Charlotte, a mysterious girl that seems attracted to Alice. After a one-night stand, Alice is left wondering who Charlotte was. But not only that. She feels sick and tired, unable to meet the daylight coming through the window. Alice knows something is not right and can't really explain what it is. She needs to find Charlotte.

Thanks to Bram Stoker's Dracula, we know María, Charlotte and Alice are vampires, yet it is not explicitly stated. We get to know about their powers, about their weaknesses, about their thirst for blood, about their loneliness, and about how all three are related to each other. I won't say much more to avoid spoiling the book.

One cool detail about the audiobook is that it is read by three women, each one giving life to every character in a fantastic way. Marisa Calin is María, a great performance for sure. The mix of Spanish and English words was fun to listen to. Katie Leung (Cho Chang in the original Harry Potter movies) is Alice, a delightful performance i really enjoyed. And finally, Julia Whelan is Charlotte, maybe the most entertaining reader of the three.

Final rating: 4 midnight roses out of 5.
