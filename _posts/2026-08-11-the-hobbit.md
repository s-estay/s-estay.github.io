I have this memory from when I was around 14 years old, hanging with friends after school. Two of them were talking about J.R.R. Tolkien’s books, how great they were, which were their favourites, and so on. I remember them discussing The Silmarillion as the best book of the Middle-Earth series. I lied, saying, “Yeah, sure, that book is great,” unashamed of not having read it, nor looking forward to doing so. I wasn’t especially into books at that age; all I wanted to do was run and jump around, meet new people, and feel like I belonged. It wasn’t until recently that I learned The Silmarillion, unlike The Hobbit, is a challenging read. And it would take me almost 30 years to finally open a J.R.R. Tolkien book, not to impress anyone, but to satisfy my own curiosity.

The Hobbit is a children’s book, yet it is wonderfully accessible in its use of language, funny, and exciting. Though we are introduced to a world of magic and magical beings, no grand mythological questions are posed. We simply accept that Gandalf is a wizard and Galadriel an elf, not allegories or symbols of anything deeper.

One of the things I liked most about the book is the transformation of Bilbo Baggins, from a hobbit content with his peaceful life to one who taps into an inner adventurous spirit inherited from an old relative. His journey challenges him to confront the unknown, the unpleasant, and the dangerous. Readers can see in Bilbo a reflection of their own potential, proof that even the most unassuming among us can rise to meet the extraordinary when pushed beyond the familiar.

I can’t say much more than that The Hobbit is a great book, an icon of English literature, and one everyone should read at least once. And if you don’t feel like reading, I cannot recommend enough the silky, evocative voice of Andy Serkis, which breathes life into every character, giving the story a new dimension. His performance is vivid, colourful, and utterly immersive, especially his chilling, unforgettable Gollum.

Final verdict: Four rings of power out of five.
