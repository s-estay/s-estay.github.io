One of my penpals is a Harry Potter fan. After telling her about how much I liked the first book, she told me, *"I'm always surprised at people that haven't read HP"*, which is totally fair, as HP was a cultural shock around the world. To this day, I still can't explain why it took me this long to read this book, even though I thought some tiny bit of curiosity should have existed in 1997, 13 years old and certainly capable of reading, or later as a teenager in 2001, when the first movie was released. I was most likely living under a rock.

It was my recent trip to the Highlands of Scotland that sparked my curiosity for the magical world of Harry Potter. I can't tell you how many times I came across a Harry Potter reference, like the Glenfinnan Viaduct or the memorabilia boutiques in Edinburgh’s Victoria Street. Even as often as there were references to the TV series Outlander (of which I barely managed to watch season one, a gore feast for sure). Right after returning home, I binge-watched all the movies, most pleased by the Highlands scenery, slowly morphing into a Potterhead.

*The Philosopher’s Stone* is indeed a great book. So many iconic moments, one after the other, keeping me entertained from start to finish. Not many books can do that. And with the movie already loaded in my mind, it was fun to see all the threads and hidden intentions presented in plain sight, a cheat code to better understand complex characters like Snape and to give more background to those who didn’t get enough screen time, like Hagrid.

Stephen Fry is great as an audiobook reader. That alone is reason enough to give it a go. For me, this book deserves five sorting hats out of five, a classic!
